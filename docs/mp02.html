Mini-Project 2: Identifying Environmentally Responsible US Public Transit Systems

In an effort to curb the ongoing global warming crisis, it is more important now than ever that we pay attention to our carbon emissions. Public transportation is a great way to reduce our collective’s carbon footprint. Using data from the National Transit Database (NTD) and the Energy Information Administration (EIA), we can evaluate the fuel usage and carbon emissions of Americas public transportation agencies, and determine who is the “greenest”.

Step 1: Data Acquisition

We will be analyzing data from 3 datasets. One will be the State Electricity Profiles from the EIA. The other two datasets are from the NTD, one with energy consumption by transportation agency and mode, the other will have info on transportation services offered by the same transportation agencies.   Acquiring State Electricity Profiles

get_eia_sep <- function(state, abbr){
    library(tidyverse)
    library(stringr)
    library(httr2)
    library(rvest)
    library(fs)
  
    state_formatted <- str_to_lower(state) |> str_replace_all("\\s", "")
    
    dir_name <- fs::path("data", "mp02")
    file_name <- fs::path(dir_name, state_formatted)
    
    fs::dir_create(dir_name, recurse=TRUE)
    
    if(!fs::file_exists(file_name)){
        BASE_URL <- "https://www.eia.gov"
        RESPONSE <- request(BASE_URL) |> 
          req_url_path("electricity", 
                       "state", 
                       state_formatted) |>
          req_headers(
            `User-Agent` = "Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; Intel Mac OS X 10.15; rv:149.0) Gecko/20100101 Firefox/149.0"
            ) |>   
          req_retry(
                max_tries = 4, 
          ) |>
          req_perform(file_name)
    
        resp_check_status(RESPONSE)
    }
    
    TABLE <- read_html(file_name) |> 
        html_element("table") |> 
        html_table() |>
        mutate(Item = str_to_lower(Item))
    
    if("U.S. rank" %in% colnames(TABLE)){
        TABLE <- TABLE |> rename(Rank = `U.S. rank`)
    }
    
    CO2_MWh <- TABLE |> 
        filter(Item == "carbon dioxide (lbs/mwh)") |>
        pull(Value) |> 
        str_replace_all(",", "") |>
        as.numeric()
    
    PRIMARY <- TABLE |> 
        filter(Item == "primary energy source") |> 
        pull(Rank)
    
    RATE <- TABLE |>
        filter(Item == "average retail price (cents/kwh)") |>
        pull(Value) |>
        as.numeric()
    
    GENERATION_MWh <- TABLE |>
        filter(Item == "net generation (megawatthours)") |>
        pull(Value) |>
        str_replace_all(",", "") |>
        as.numeric()
    
    data.frame(CO2_MWh               = CO2_MWh, 
               primary_source        = PRIMARY,
               electricity_price_MWh = RATE * 10, # / 100 cents to dollars &
               # * 1000 kWh to MWH 
               generation_MWh        = GENERATION_MWh, 
               state                 = state, 
               abbreviation          = abbr
    )
}

EIA_SEP_REPORT <- map2(state.name, state.abb, get_eia_sep) |> list_rbind()

load_ntd_energy <- function(){
  library(readxl)
  library(httr2)
  library(tidyverse)
  library(fs)
  
  # Create 'data/mp02' directory if not already present
  DATA_DIR <- fs::path("data", "mp02")
  fs::dir_create(DATA_DIR, recurse=TRUE)

  NTD_ENERGY_FILE <- fs::path(DATA_DIR, "2025_ntd_energy.xlsx")

  if(!fs::file_exists(NTD_ENERGY_FILE)){
    library(httr2)
  
    request("https://www.transit.dot.gov/") |>
      req_url_path("sites",
                   "fta.dot.gov",
                   "files",
                   "2026-09",
                   "2025 Energy Consumption_260806.xlsx") |> 
      req_headers(
        `User-Agent` = "Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; Intel Mac OS X 10.15; rv:149.0) Gecko/20100101 Firefox/149.0") |> 
      req_perform(NTD_ENERGY_FILE)
  }
  
  if(file.info(NTD_ENERGY_FILE)$size < 1e4){
        cat("I was unable to download the NTD Energy File. Please try again.\n")
        stop("Download failed. Please delete the corrupted file by running `unlink('data/mp02/2025_ntd_energy.xlsx')` and try again.")
    }

  to_numeric_fill_0 <- function(x){
    x <- if_else(x == "-", NA, x)
    replace_na(as.numeric(x), 0)
  }

  read_excel(NTD_ENERGY_FILE) |> 
    select(-c(`Reporter Type`, 
              `Reporting Module`, 
              `Gallons of Other Fuel`, 
              `Other Fuel Description`)) |>
    mutate(across(-c(`Agency Name`, 
                     `Mode`,
                     `TOS`), 
                  to_numeric_fill_0)) |>
    group_by(`NTD ID`, `Mode`, `Agency Name`) |>
    summarize(across(where(is.numeric), sum), 
              .groups = "keep") |>
    mutate(ENERGY = sum(c_across(c(where(is.numeric))))) |>
    filter(ENERGY > 0) |>
    select(-ENERGY) |>
    ungroup()
}

NTD_ENERGY <- load_ntd_energy()

load_ntd_service <- function(){
  library(httr2)
  library(tidyverse)
  library(fs)
  
  # Create 'data/mp02' directory if not already present
  DATA_DIR <- fs::path("data", "mp02")
  fs::dir_create(DATA_DIR, recurse=TRUE)

  NTD_SERVICE_FILE <- fs::path(DATA_DIR, "2025_ntd_service.csv")

  if(!fs::file_exists(NTD_SERVICE_FILE)){
    library(httr2)
  
    request("https://data.transportation.gov/resource/4fir-qbim.csv") |>
      req_url_query(`$query`=paste(
        "SELECT  `_5_digit_ntd_id` AS `NTD_ID`", 
        "`max_agency` AS `AGENCY`",
        "`max_reporter_type` AS `reporter_type`",
        "`max_organization_type` AS `organization_type`",
        "`max_city` AS `city`",
        "`max_state` AS `state`",
        "`mode`",
        "`type_of_service`",
        "`max_primary_uza_code` as `uza_code`",
        "`max_primary_uza_name` as `uza_name`",
        "`max_primary_uza_area_sq_miles` as `uza_sq_miles`",
        "`max_primary_uza_population` as `uza_population`",
        "`max_service_area_sq_miles` as `service_area_sq_miles`",
        "`max_service_area_population` as `service_area_population`",
        "`questionable_record` as `flagged_likely_inaccurate`",
        "`sum_unlinked_passenger_trips_upt` as `total_upt`",
        "`sum_passenger_miles` WHERE caseless_one_of(`report_year`, '2025') limit 50000", 
        sep=",")) |>
      req_headers(
        `User-Agent` = "Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; Intel Mac OS X 10.15; rv:149.0) Gecko/20100101 Firefox/149.0") |> 
      req_perform(NTD_SERVICE_FILE)
  }
  
  if(file.info(NTD_SERVICE_FILE)$size < 1e4){
        cat("I was unable to download the NTD Service File. Please try again.\n")
        stop("Download failed. Please delete the corrupted file by running `unlink('data/mp02/2025_ntd_service.xlsx')` and try again.")
    }

  read_csv(NTD_SERVICE_FILE) |>
    mutate(flagged_likely_inaccurate = !is.na(flagged_likely_inaccurate))
}

NTD_SERVICE <- load_ntd_service()

Step 4: Joining the Three Data Files

Now that we’ve explored the three datasets and all they have to offer, it is time to combine the best parts of them in order to have a singular dataset to use for our final awards.

The NTD Energy dataset is probably the most important, since we will need all of the information on fuel usage by agency and mode to determine how green an agency is. In order to keep all of the rows from this table, we will start with NTD Energy, and join the other two datasets to it.

Before we can join any data from NTD Service, we need to clean it up a bit. For starters, the ID column is currently a character, when it should be numeric. More importantly, each row in the NTD Service table represents a specific transportation service provided by an agency, where as each row in the NTD Energy chart represents a transportation mode provided by an agency. This sounds very similar, because it is.

According to the NTD, each agency offers multiple modes of transportation (Heavy Rail, Demand Response, Ferryboat, etc.). Within those modes, there can be multiple types of services. Directly Operated, Purchased Transportation, and Taxi. What does this mean for us? We need to aggregate the data for each service to ensure there is one row for each agency x mode pairing. Otherwise, we will potentially lose data.

Once this is done however, we can do two left joins to join the NTD Service dataset to the NTD Energy dataset, and then the EIA Report to the combined Energy + Service dataset.

Show Code 
NTD_SERVICE_agg <- NTD_SERVICE |>
  mutate(NTD_ID = as.numeric(NTD_ID)) |> #need to convert NTD_ID in service table to dbl, can not join a dbl and a character column

EIA_ENERGY_SERVICE <- ENERGY_SERVICE |>
  left_join(EIA_SEP_REPORT |> select(CO2_MWh, abbreviation), by = c("state" = "abbreviation"))

Step 5: Calculating Emissions

Now that our table is joined, we need to calculate the total emissions for each Agency x Mode pair.

This will be done by multiplying each fuel source by a carbon co-efficient in order to convert the combustion fuels to CO2 emissions. The list of carbon coefficients an be found on the EPA website, on page 2 of this document on 2025 Emission Factors.

For electric fuel sources (Kilowatt hours of Electric Battery and Electric Propulsion), we are going to divide the number by 1,000 (to convert kilowatt (kWh) to megawatt (MWh)), and then multiple that by the state CO2 emission value provided in the EIA Report (Pounds of CO2 Emitted Per MWh of Electricity Produced).

For kilograms of hydrogen, we are actually going to multiply by 0. Hydrogen does not release any C02 emissions. Instead, it releases Nitrogen Oxide when burned or combusted, which is also bad but not the topic of this assignment!

Lastly, we are going to exclude empty columns from our calculations, such as Gallons of Bunker Fuel and Gallons of Kerosene. As stated before in our Exploratory Data Analysis, none of the agencies used these fuels. We are also going to rearrange the columns in the final table to make it easier to read.

The final table should look something like this:

Step 6: Normalizing Emissions to Transit Usage

The agencies in our database come in all different sizes. It would be unfair to compare the efficiency of a rural town’s bus network, to a larger transportation network like the MTA Subway or the LA Metro. Because of this, we are creating a normalized table, that will aggregate all of the services each agency offers, and sort them into size groups based on their total UPT.

We will also add columns to display the total fuel usage by agency, total CO2 emission, CO2 emissions per unlinked passenger trip, and CO2 emissions per passenger mile. This will help us determine which agencies are the most green (relative to their size), and will determine who gets our very prestigious awards.

normalized_emissions_by_agency <- EIA_ENERGY_SERVICE |>
  group_by(`NTD ID`, `Agency Name`, state) |>
  summarise(total_fuel_by_agency = sum(total_fuel_by_mode),
            total_em_by_agency = sum(total_CO2_em_by_mode),
            total_upt_by_agency = sum(upt_total_by_mode), 
            total_mile_by_agency = sum(pass_miles_total_by_mode),

green_award_winners <- ranked_emissions_by_agency |>
  group_by(agency_size) |>
  mutate(
    #calculate "green score" by using min_rank to give 1 to the lowest emissions
    rank_co2_trip = min_rank(emission_per_upt),
    rank_co2_mile = min_rank(emission_per_pass_mile), 
    green_score = rank_co2_trip + rank_co2_mile
  ) |>
  slice_min(green_score, n = 1, with_ties = FALSE) |>
  ungroup()

Final Press Release

Today, I (the Executive Director of the Green Transit Alliance) am announcing the recipients of the Annual Green Transit Alliance (GTA) Awards, recognizing transportation agencies that demonstrated extraordinary efforts in emissions reduction and energy efficiency in 2025.

This year’s evaluation analyzed data from 508 transportation agencies, offering a total of 1134 methods of transportation. In order to keep our awards fair, we are separating agencies into Small, Medium, and Large size tiers based on their annual amount of unlinked passenger trips.

Honoring the “Greenest Agencies”

The Greenest Agency Award recognizes agencies that successfully ranked among the top performers in both CO2 emissions per unlinked passenger trip and CO2 emissions per passenger mile within their respective size groups. Each agency was given a “green score” based on their rank within the two categories, and the winner had the best green score of their size group.

Large Agency Winner: City of Culver City (CA)
City of Culver City emits 0.012 lbs of CO2 per trip and 0.004 lbs per passenger mile.

Medium Agency Winner: City of Torrance (CA)
With 0.053 lbs of CO2 per trip and 0.010 lbs per passenger mile.

Small Agency Winner: Mid Mon Valley Transit Authority (PA)
Mid Mon Valley Transit Authority emits 0.009 lbs of CO2 per trip and 0.001 lbs per passenger mile.

All of these agencies report emissions well below the national median of 2.399 lbs of CO2 per trip and 0.424 lbs of CO2 per passenger mile.

The Best Fuel Efficiency Award highlights agencies that extract maximum movement from minimal energy resources. Winners were determined by calculating the lowest ratio of total fuel consumed per passenger mile.
